Artifact 01 • First audience-specific artifact

AI and Machine Learning: A Timeline of Development and Impact

Target audience: Employers, business professionals, educators, and learners who want a clear introduction to how AI developed and why its milestones matter.

A visual overview of major eras
1950sEarly foundations of machine intelligence and the Turing Test.
1956Dartmouth workshop helps establish artificial intelligence as a field.
1980sExpert systems bring rule-based AI into practical settings.
1990sData-driven machine learning advances; Deep Blue defeats Garry Kasparov in 1997.
2010sDeep learning breakthroughs expand image, speech, and language applications.
2016AlphaGo demonstrates powerful learning-based performance in Go.
2020sLarge language models and generative AI reach broad public use.
TodayGrowing focus on responsible, reliable, and human-centered AI.

Timeline is an educational overview, not an exhaustive history. Dates and descriptions should be checked against the final course artifact and cited sources before public submission.

Introduction

This artifact presents a concise visual overview of important developments in artificial intelligence and machine learning. It is designed to help a nontechnical audience understand that today’s AI systems emerged through decades of research, experimentation, and changing approaches.

Description

The timeline connects foundational ideas, early AI research, expert systems, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI. It emphasizes the progression of the field and encourages viewers to consider both the capabilities and responsibilities associated with newer technologies.

Objective

To organize key AI/ML milestones into an accessible resource and demonstrate the ability to summarize technical history for a general professional audience.

Process

Identify major periods and representative milestones; arrange them chronologically; summarize each in plain language; select a visual format; and review the content for clarity, accuracy, and audience relevance. The published course artifact should be attached or linked here as tangible evidence.
